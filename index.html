Bears say NO to Hammond: "We're Staying in Illinois, Even If We Have to Play in a Target Parking Lot"
In a stunning reversal, the Chicago Bears have completely scrapped their Indiana stadium plans, declaring the border off-limits and committing to the Land of Lincoln.
By Chuck "The Scoop" Kowalski | Senior NFL Insider

CHICAGO, IL — In a move that has left lawmakers, fans, and real estate developers utterly bewildered, the Chicago Bears have officially pulled the plug on their highly publicized relocation to Hammond, Indiana. Despite the team's relentless push over the last several months to cross state lines, ownership has had a sudden and dramatic change of heart.

The realization, according to team insiders, hit upper management late Thursday night: moving to Indiana was simply too ridiculous to stomach.

"Sources tell me the Bears woke up in a cold sweat. They realized you can't be the 'Chicago' Bears if your stadium requires passing three toll booths and a fireworks megastore just to get to a tailgate."
— Adam Shafter, NFL Network Insider

According to Shafter, the Bears' front office shredded the Indiana blueprints this morning. The team has committed to staying in Illinois no matter what the cost, even if it means resorting to drastic measures. The organization is reportedly looking at all options, including staying at Soldier Field, returning to the Arlington Heights property, or simply setting up bleachers in the parking lot of the Schaumburg Target.

"Indiana is completely off the table," an anonymous team official stated. "We tried the whole 'Northwest Indiana' rebranding pitch, but deep dish pizza just doesn't taste the same when you cross the border. It's a matter of principle. We are an Illinois team. We will play in a muddy field in Naperville before we cross state lines."

The sudden reversal is a massive blow to Indiana officials, who had reportedly already prepared a welcome parade featuring 500 tractors and a giant pork tenderloin sandwich. Meanwhile, Illinois politicians are scrambling to figure out if the Bears are serious this time, or if this is just another incredibly complex negotiating tactic to get free landscaping at the lakefront.

Whatever the reasoning, one thing is clear: the Monsters of the Midway are refusing to become the Monsters of the Midwest. They are staying home.
